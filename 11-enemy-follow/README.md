# How to Make an Enemy Follow the Player in Godot 4

The project of the Phaix mini tutorial "How to Make an Enemy Follow the Player in Godot 4". Open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7 and press F5: a goblin runs after the knight, and the knight walks away from him with the arrow keys. He is faster than the goblin, so he can run away, and when he stops the goblin catches up and stands next to him.

`enemy.gd` is the lesson: a `CharacterBody2D` with two exported slots, `speed` and `target` (the node to follow). In `_physics_process` the goblin takes `global_position.direction_to(target.global_position)`, the way to the target with a length of one, multiplies it by `speed` and calls `move_and_slide()`, which also stops him at walls and at the knight. The Enemy's Target slot holds the knight in `main.tscn`. There is no path finding: he walks straight at the knight, and a wall between them stops him (navigation is another topic).

Code and scenes are MIT, see `LICENSE` in the root of the repo.

The knight and the goblin animate from `knight_frames.tres` and `goblin_frames.tres` (idle and walk) and `knight_sprite.gd` on their AnimatedSprite2D: walk while the body really moves, idle while it stands or is held. The script reads `get_real_velocity()`, the movement that happened, and not `velocity`, the velocity the body asks for: `enemy.gd` sets `velocity` every frame, also while the knight or a wall holds the goblin, and he stands there and plays idle.

The art is 16x16 DungeonTileset II by 0x72 (https://0x72.itch.io/dungeontileset-ii), CC0, see `art/LICENSE.txt`.

`tests/run_tests.gd` is the project's check (`godot --headless --path . -s tests/run_tests.gd`): the Target slot holds the knight, the goblin walks at 60 px/s at every distance, stands next to the knight without jitter and plays idle there, follows him when he moves, the knight plays idle when a wall holds him, and stops at the wall of `short/wall.tscn`; the Short's two scenes prove the bug (the vector to the target as the velocity: fast far away, slow up close) and the fix.

`short/` holds the demos: `wall.tscn` (a wall hangs from the top wall, the goblin stands at it), and the scenes of the Short, `bug.tscn` with `enemy_bug.gd` (the vector to the target times 0.25 as the velocity) and `fix.tscn` with the project's own enemy, each with a readout of the goblin's speed at the top right: `bug.tscn` the length of his velocity, a quarter of the distance between the two centres (it counts down while he crawls in and reads 14 once he stands at the knight: `enemy_bug.tscn` is Floating like the video's enemy, so he keeps asking for the vector while the knight's shape holds his body; it sits on layer 2 and looks at layer 1, so the knight does not feel him), `fix.tscn` the set speed, the exported `speed` of `enemy.gd` (he stands at the knight at the end of the take: his velocity still reads 60 or 120 there while his body hardly moves, so the readout says what is set; in `fix.tscn` the goblin starts at the left edge of the frame at almost the knight's height, walks in from the side and stands beside the knight, idle, not over his head). The folder is not needed for the lesson.
